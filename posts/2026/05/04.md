sidestepping the nebulous magnetism of the uncanny robot-child discourse that has tided over *Pragmata*'s recent release for a moment, there's another oddity in its reception that confuses me. during [Skill Up's review of *Pragmata*](https://youtu.be/6n84HC57zEU), Ralph remarks that "\[it's\] a video game-ass video game" in a positive manner. he reiterates this sentiment a few more times, saying "its most endearing quality is how unashamed it is to just be a good old-fashioned video game" and "the result is a tight, focused, clever, novel video game that is defined by how video-gamey it feels". haha. beyond that review from SkillUp though, a cursory glance at both media coverage and early player reception surrounding *Pragmata* reveals this language about the game to be fairly common (again, step around the robot-child discourse vortex, we don't have time). from [Xbox Wire's article](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2026/03/18/pragmata-hands-on-preview-boss-weapons/) entitled "Pragmata Loves Being a Video Game" to [Reddit gamers](https://www.reddit.com/r/PS5pro/comments/1ss0ghr/pragmata_is_an_oasis_in_the_middle_of_desert_for/) praising its "arcade-like", "video-gamey feel", and [IGN's own review](https://www.ign.com/articles/pragmata-review) also dubbing it a "video game-ass video game", i have to admit i'm just a little lost. what does all that mean, really?

apparently, the phrase is used to classify games that are primarily focused on systems and structures endemic to the medium— stuff you wouldn't be able find outside the scope of video games. for as much as this language is leveled at *Pragmata* as an aspect of the game to be praised and appreciated, the exact reason this distinction matters or contributes to its quality is often left missing. instead, what usually succeeds is a laundry list of game elements and features that a reviewer might've found intriguing: a hub-area, collectibles, currencies, character upgrades, linear platforming, etc., and very little ends up being said in critical assessment of these elements. this all adds up to make for a pretty frustrating reading experience: yes, we know that *Pragmata* is a video game, maybe more ostensibly than some others; now what?

![](./content/04-cover.jpg)

*Pragmata* may be a game more structurally reliant on explicit, transparent ludic elements than titles like *Disco Elysium* or Telltale's *The Walking Dead*, hence the "video-gamey" feel, but i'd argue shifting those games away from narrative focus would detract from their strengths, which again highlights the missing connective tissue between the distinction and critical analysis. these are conclusions i could draw on my own after watching a 30-minute gameplay showcase of *Pragmata*. video game analysts and writers are leaving their subject on the table uncut, instead choosing to poke and prod at it with high-level classifications and designations.

this peculiarity matters because it points to a stagnation in broader critical video games discussion. game reviews, especially those from mainstream publications, are typically the main source of writing on video games the average audience is exposed to. so too, will casual gamers poke and prod around the core mechanics and systems of a game to rate high-level designations on a sliding scale (i.e. the aforementioned Reddit comments). the nuance is notably shallower in conversations about video games compared to that of avid readers discussing a novel they might like, where grade school language classes and better familiarity with critical engagement with narrative mediums foster more criticisms to be made with greater specificity.

to be clear, this isn't an judgement of intelligence or critical thinking skills, but one of poor toolsets. the language here isn't wrong, but rather shallow, and makes for a poor critical approach. we can't stop at identification and call it analysis. it's important for this type of writing to be especially diligent in its engagement with the subject matter if we are to foster the kinds of critical discussions that properly reflect the artistry and craft found in video games. gamers deserve game *reviews*, not just reports.
